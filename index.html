AI systems work.

Four open-source systems that put models and agents to work under rules: scoped tools, measured accuracy, human approval where it matters. Every figure opens its source, and every case study says what isn’t finished.

01

Agent Perimeter

Scans the tools an AI agent can reach and proves — not guesses — which ones an attacker could hijack.

It flags suspicious text, then proves whether attacker-controlled text can reach a privileged tool; you run your own agent against a canary to see whether it obeys. No agent is bundled and no model is called. Every published number can be regenerated by someone who doesn’t trust it.

Ground Truth

03

Ledger Sense

Document intelligence with confidence routing: reads construction invoices, checks the arithmetic, and only lets a human approve anything that moves money. The model lane refuses anything above redacted sensitivity. An engineering prototype; my own audit of it is in the case study.

Deterministic-first architecture: Decimal money handling (no floats in the money path), sensitivity boundaries enforced structurally, writeback that never approves or pays, and a hard stop on bank-detail changes that only an attested human confirmation can clear.

04

Selector Drift

Self-healing extraction for business systems with no API: targets are described in language, re-resolved through a five-rung cascade that ends in visual grounding, and no heal is trusted until it passes a validation gate. In development.

The heal log is the product: a wrong heal silently returns plausible, incorrect numbers into an accounting system for weeks — so the validation gate, not the heal, is the centre of the design.

05

AZTDP

Scores how every request behaves, not only whether its login token is valid, so a stolen token gets stopped instead of trusted.

The check runs synchronously on the request path, before any business logic: IP drift, geo drift, device change, request-rate anomalies and an ML anomaly score decide the outcome, and a token seen from a new IP within 60 seconds is hard-denied even in fail-open mode.

How a security review works.

Booked through Upwork. The report arrives 3 to 7 days after I have access and the scope is signed.

  1. Step 1

    You tell me your stack and what worries you

    Message me on Upwork. On weekdays I usually reply within 24 hours with the first three things I’d check.

  2. Step 2

    We agree a written scope

    You sign a short scope and authorisation form before I test anything, and I work on staging or a copy. If production is all you have, we agree read-only checks and a time window first. NDA on request.

  3. Step 3

    I sign in as each role and try to cross the line

    Row-level security, keys, payments, webhooks, and every AI feature and tool an agent can call. Anything I can reproduce gets a script you can re-run; the rest gets exact steps and screenshots.

  4. Step 4

    You get the report, the fixes and a recheck

    Each finding is ranked Critical to Low, with plain-English impact, the reproduction and a code or policy diff. The report lists what I didn’t test. I remove my access within 7 days of delivery. One recheck within 14 days is included; if you want me to apply the fixes, that’s $40 an hour, with no warranty beyond the recheck.
